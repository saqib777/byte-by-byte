*Synthetic Data

What it is: Data generated by algorithms, simulations, or generative models (rather than collected from real-world events) that statistically resembles real data without exposing any real individual's information.

Why it matters: It solves two problems at once — (1) privacy: you can share or test with data that carries no real personal information, and (2) scarcity: you can generate more examples of rare events that real data doesn't have enough of.

Types:

Fully synthetic — every value is generated by a model/simulation; no real records used at all.
Partially synthetic — real records are kept, but specific sensitive fields (names, IDs, financial details) are replaced with synthetically generated values.
Hybrid synthetic — real records and fully synthetic records are combined into one dataset, commonly to boost rare-event classes (fraud, rare disease cases, edge-case failures) for model training.

How it's generated: Statistical resampling, rule-based simulation, or generative models like GANs and VAEs (for more complex, realistic data like images or tabular data with intricate correlations).

Risk to know: Synthetic data trades off fidelity (how closely it matches real patterns) against privacy (how little it leaks about real individuals) — the closer it mimics real data, the higher the re-identification risk if not done carefully.

Career relevance for you: This is directly useful in SDET/QA — generating realistic-looking test data (user records, transactions, API payloads) without touching real customer data, which is often a hard requirement under privacy laws like GDPR when testing against production-like environments.
